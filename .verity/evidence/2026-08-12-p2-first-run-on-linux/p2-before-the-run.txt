THE QUESTION ONLY CI CAN ANSWER, AND THIS MACHINE CANNOT: DO TWELVE FIXTURES WRITTEN ON DARWIN
HOLD THEIR DECLARED STATE ON LINUX?

Every state in the table above was observed on one host, of one architecture, with one shell, one
libc, one filesystem, and one set of userland tools -- and the two userlands differ where these
fixtures live. This is not a general anxiety about portability; below are the lines that carry
the exposure, and the ones that do not.

  AT RISK -- shasum is not coreutils. tests/bypass_fe:145 gates on `command -v shasum` and calls
  it at :176 and :413-414; tests/bypass_f8 calls it at :161 and :181; scripts/detect_tamper.sh
  calls it at :19 as the whole of its verdict. shasum is a perl script, not a GNU coreutils
  binary, and nothing in this tree asserts that the runner carries perl -- unlike shellcheck and
  gitleaks, which are fetched by digest, and unlike node, which at least has a probe. If the
  image ever drops perl, f8 and fe both exit 2 at once: fe routes to `fail` and f8 to
  `unmeasured`, so the same missing tool produces one FAIL and one unattrib, and the run exits 1
  with a line that says shasum only for fe. E1/E2/E3 all fail together.

  AT RISK -- the wall clock on a shared runner, and it is exactly two files, not the four I would
  have guessed. tests/parallel_claim_fixture.sh:63-64 sets REPORT_BUDGET=20 and HOLDER_BUDGET=10,
  polled in 0.05s steps at :149-153 and :246-248: it spawns concurrent claimers and gives each 20
  seconds to record an exit code. On a loaded two-core runner that budget is the tightest thing
  in the suite. Its failure is honest -- UNATTRIB, exit 2, counted as `unmeasured` at
  run_tests.sh:657-658 -- so it costs TESTS INCONCLUSIVE and exit 2, never a false accusation.
  tests/render_session_fixture.sh:339-343 is the dangerous one: 60 iterations of `sleep 0.1` is a
  six-second ceiling on `--follow` rendering appended events, and its call site (run_tests.sh:803)
  is a bare `|| fail=1`. A slow runner therefore produces `FAIL [--follow must render events
  appended after start]` -- an ACCUSATION against the renderer, produced by a budget, with
  nothing in the line to say the clock is what moved. Of everything in this document that is the
  observation most likely to be misread.

  NOT AT RISK, AND MEASURED HERE -- adr010 is not a wall-clock fixture. tests/adr010_refusal_
  fixture.sh contains no sleep, no budget and no clock read; its exit 2 is a setup failure. Nor
  are the two lease fixtures: run_tests.sh:679-680 records that lease_window reconstructs its
  window with a held descriptor, and :700-703 that lease_expiry hands the predecessor's record
  through a FIFO -- both say in their own text that their verdict cannot move with load, and the
  sources agree. adr010 has a different Linux exposure and it is a small one: :157 runs
  `sort -u` with no LC_ALL, and under a UTF-8 locale `sort -u` can treat lines differing only in
  ignorable characters as duplicates. The five lines it deduplicates are identical ASCII JSON, so
  the collapse it could cause would be a MASKED non-determinism rather than a false red: DISTINCT
  would read 1 without having earned it. Lowest weight of anything here, and named because a
  locale that hides a defect is worth more attention than one that invents it.

  NOT AT RISK, AND ANTICIPATED IN THE SOURCE -- mktemp. Every scratch dir is templated:
  run_tests.sh:11 and :59 onward, and each fixture's own WORK= line, all
  `mktemp -d "${TMPDIR:-/tmp}/hp-*.XXXXXX"`. BSD mktemp -d with no template and GNU mktemp -d
  with no template disagree, which is why no call site has one. TMPDIR being unset on the runner
  is handled by the default in the same expansion.

  NOT AT RISK -- sed and stat. The only sed in tests/ and scripts/ is `sed -n 's/^KEY=//p'`
  against the two pin files (ci.yml:22-24, run_tests.sh:981-982): POSIX BRE, identical under BSD
  and GNU. There is no `sed -i`, no `-E`, no `-r` anywhere in either tree, and `stat` is not
  called at all -- so the two most reliable BSD/GNU tripwires are simply not present.

  AT RISK, JOINTLY -- comm, git ls-files, and a case-sensitive filesystem. run_tests.sh:898-899
  compares the register against `git ls-files -- 'tests/bypass_*'` with `LC_ALL=C comm -13/-23`
  over two `LC_ALL=C sort`ed sides, which is the correct shape: the collation is pinned and both
  directions are accused separately. Measured on this host at this basis, with the same
  invocation: the registered set and the tracked set are byte-for-byte equal, twelve and twelve.
  What that measurement CANNOT settle is the filesystem underneath it. APFS here is
  case-insensitive; ext4 there is not, and a fresh Linux clone has core.ignorecase false. A path
  literal at :853-874 differing from the tracked name in case alone is invisible on this host and
  is two accusations on that one. Nothing says it does -- the comm above found nothing -- but
  this host structurally cannot look, and F5 is the shape the answer takes.

  AT RISK, WEAKLY -- the shell itself. Every fixture runs under GNU bash 3.2.57 here and bash 5.x
  there, and the direction is the safe one: a construct that runs under 3.2 runs under 5.x, so
  the exposure is behavioural rather than syntactic. What remains is not the syntax but the
  machine: two cores instead of many, x86_64 instead of arm64, a cold page cache, and every
  process in the suite sharing them. That is the same exposure as the wall-clock section above,
  arriving by a different road, and it is the reason parallel_claim was given three outcomes
  instead of two.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS FOR. Not to be right. If E1-E10 all hold, this file is worth very little --
it says a suite that passed on one host passed on another. Its value is entirely in the branches
where it does not hold: because the states were read off the register rather than off a run,
because the falsifiers are enumerated before the log exists, and because the node line is
recorded as an unknown rather than as a guess, a red in CI can be attributed to a named line here
rather than absorbed as "the first Linux run needed some fixing". That attribution is the only
thing this file buys, and it is only purchasable in advance.
